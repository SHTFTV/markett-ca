# How to Compare Home Heating Quotes Without Guessing

A heating quote should explain what your home needs and what the contractor will deliver. A brand name and a single price leave too many decisions hidden. Before comparing proposals, give every contractor the same description of your home, comfort problems and plans for renovations.

## Start with the rooms that are uncomfortable

Write down where the problem happens, when it happens and what you notice. A bedroom that stays cold while the main floor overheats is a different conversation from a whole home that struggles on the coldest days. Include changes since the existing equipment was installed: an addition, new windows, attic insulation or a finished basement. These observations help frame an assessment; they do not diagnose the equipment.

## Ask how the equipment was sized

Natural Resources Canada recommends obtaining at least three quotes and having the home's heating requirements calculated. Ask each contractor to show the calculation behind the proposed capacity. Do not assume the old appliance's rating is the right replacement size. If a heat pump is proposed, ask how local winter conditions, the home's heating load and any supplementary heating are addressed.

## Compare the same installation scope

Use this worksheet when requesting written proposals:

| Item | What to ask for |
| --- | --- |
| Equipment | Exact indoor and outdoor model numbers, as applicable |
| Sizing | Heating-load calculation and the assumptions used |
| Distribution | Included duct, airflow or hydronic work |
| Controls | Thermostat, setup and explanation of operating modes |
| Site work | Electrical, venting, drainage and access work included |
| Administration | Who handles applicable permits and inspections |
| Completion | Startup checks, documentation and homeowner demonstration |
| Aftercare | Labour warranty, manufacturer warranty and service arrangements |

Ask contractors to mark exclusions explicitly. If electrical work is an allowance rather than a firm amount, ask what must happen before that amount becomes final. Keep each proposal's assumptions beside its total so you are comparing the same job.

## Separate running-cost estimates from guarantees

Request the energy rates, expected heating demand and equipment performance used in any savings estimate. A percentage without those assumptions is difficult to evaluate. Ask whether ongoing service costs and any backup heat are included. Rebate eligibility should be checked against the current program and exact equipment before ordering; an advertised incentive is not a promise of approval.

## Agree on the handover

Before signing, ask what records you will receive and whom to contact if comfort is poor after installation. A useful handover includes model information, manuals, warranty details and a clear explanation of routine owner care. Gas, electrical and refrigerant work belongs with appropriately qualified professionals.

Your next step: send the same worksheet to each contractor, then request written answers for every blank. The most useful quote is the one you can understand and compare.
