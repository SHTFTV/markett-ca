# Before your gas fireplace service visit: a homeowner’s checklist

A useful fireplace service appointment starts with good information. Before booking, gather the appliance details and describe what you have noticed. Leave diagnosis, adjustments and internal cleaning to a qualified gas contractor.

## Make a small appliance record

Record the manufacturer, model and serial number if they are accessible without removing panels or reaching near hot parts. Find the owner’s manual and previous service receipt. Note whether the fireplace is an insert, freestanding appliance or built-in unit.

Write down the symptom in plain language: when it began, whether it happens every time, and whether any recent renovation or control change preceded it. An existing photo can be useful; do not run a suspect appliance just to capture a video.

## Ask what the service includes

FortisBC’s maintenance guidance describes inspection of the burner, gas valve, ignition components, door seals, venting and air openings, along with interior glass cleaning. Ask the contractor which checks apply to your model, what the appointment includes and whether replacement parts require a separate approval. Request the completed service checklist for your records. [1]

Keep the work area accessible and arrange access with a landlord or strata manager where necessary. Explain any access restrictions before the visit rather than discovering them after the technician arrives.

## Check the model against official advisories

Use the exact model when checking recalls. A brand name alone is not enough. For example, Health Canada has a stop-use advisory for specified Insta-flame and Northern Flame direct-vent freestanding fireplaces made by CFM. The advisory identifies the affected model series; it does not apply to every gas fireplace. Follow an applicable advisory rather than waiting for routine maintenance. [2]

## Know when it is not a routine appointment

If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility’s emergency number or 911 from outside. Do not use a website contact form for an emergency. FortisBC provides this direction for customers who suspect a leak. [3]

Before the contractor leaves, ask for the findings, any restrictions on use, the next maintenance interval from the appliance instructions, and an itemized estimate for recommended repairs. Keep these with the appliance record.
