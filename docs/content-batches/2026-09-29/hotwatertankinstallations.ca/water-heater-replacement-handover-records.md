# After a Water Heater Replacement: The Records Worth Keeping

A water heater replacement is easier to manage later when the installation records are organized from day one. Before the installer leaves, request a short handover and collect the documents needed to identify the equipment, understand its care and arrange future service.

## Confirm what was actually installed

Keep the final invoice, the model and serial number, and the manufacturer's manual together. Ask the installer to identify the equipment and explain any differences from the original proposal. If a substitute model was installed, request its specifications and written agreement on the substitution.

Record the installation date and the installing business's contact information. A clear photo of an accessible equipment label can be useful; do not remove panels or enter an unsafe area to obtain one.

## Close out the agreed scope

Use the accepted quote as a checklist. Ask whether all included work is complete and whether anything requires a return visit. Request any applicable permit or inspection records, or a clear explanation of the remaining process and who is responsible. Requirements depend on the work and jurisdiction; the point is to establish the status of your installation rather than assume every job follows the same procedure.

If there are outstanding items, write down the item, responsible party and agreed follow-up. Keep those notes with the invoice instead of relying on memory.

## Understand care without taking on technical work

Ask the installer to show you the owner's manual and distinguish routine owner tasks from professional service. FortisBC recommends keeping manuals, maintaining clear space around gas appliances and using qualified contractors for service. Request a maintenance schedule appropriate to the exact model and your circumstances.

Do not ask a general online checklist to replace the manufacturer's instructions. This handover is not an invitation to adjust gas controls, venting, wiring or safety devices yourself. Ask whom to call if you notice a leak, error message or a change in hot-water performance.

## Read the warranty paperwork

Collect the manufacturer's warranty and any separate installation-labour warranty. Ask who handles a claim, what registration is needed and which documents you should retain. If registration is required, confirm who will complete it and keep the confirmation. Warranty terms vary; avoid assuming a parts warranty includes labour or every service visit.

## Build one service folder

A paper folder or a clearly named digital folder is enough. Include:

- Accepted quote and final invoice.
- Model, serial number and installation date.
- Manual and warranty documents.
- Applicable permit and inspection records.
- Handover notes and unresolved items.
- Future maintenance and repair records.

For preparation before the job, see [how to plan a hot water tank replacement](https://hotwatertankinstallation.ca/blog/plan-hot-water-tank-replacement). For ongoing care, use the [existing maintenance checklist](https://hotwatertankinstallation.ca/blog/hot-water-tank-maintenance-checklist-canada) alongside your manufacturer's manual. Good records make the next service conversation more precise.
