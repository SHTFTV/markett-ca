# Water Heater Sizing: Why First-Hour Rating Matters

A water heater can have a familiar tank size and still be a poor match for your household's busiest hour. When discussing replacement options, describe how your home uses hot water and ask the installer to explain the proposed model's delivery capacity.

## Storage volume is only one part of the picture

The capacity printed on a tank describes stored water. First-hour rating describes how much hot water a storage heater can supply during its first hour of operation under specified test conditions, starting with a heated tank. It reflects both stored hot water and the heater's ability to recover as water is used. Natural Resources Canada's Water Heater Guide uses first-hour delivery as part of the sizing discussion.

Do not treat a laboratory rating as a promise that every fixture can run together indefinitely. Installation conditions and actual demand matter. Ask the contractor to connect the specification to your household's routine.

## Map your busiest hour

Make a simple list of hot-water uses that can overlap. Include showers, a bath, kitchen cleanup and laundry only where those activities actually coincide. Note fixture flow information if available from product documentation; avoid guessing numbers just to complete the form.

| Observation | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Peak period | The hour when hot-water demand is usually highest |
| Showers and baths | How many happen close together |
| Other uses | Appliances or taps that overlap with bathing |
| Current problem | When hot water runs out and whether this is new |
| Planned changes | Additional occupants, bathroom or different fixtures |

Bring this worksheet to the installer. A sudden decline in performance may call for diagnosis rather than a larger tank.

## Ask for the actual model's rating

Request the proposed model number, storage volume and first-hour rating in the same units for every quote. NRCan's EnerGuide information identifies first-hour rating as part of how storage-water-heater capacity is represented. If a proposal lists only a nominal tank size, ask for the manufacturer's performance information before comparing it with another unit.

A useful question is: “What peak demand are you designing for, and how does this model meet it?” Follow with: “What would change if we add a bathroom?” Those questions make assumptions visible without asking you to design the installation yourself.

## Compare capacity and installation fit together

Do not select capacity independently of the proposed location, fuel or electrical supply, venting and service access. Have qualified professionals assess those details. The unit that fits the demand worksheet must also be suitable for the installation.

For background on equipment types, read the existing [storage tank versus tankless guide](https://hotwatertankinstallation.ca/blog/storage-tank-vs-tankless-water-heaters). Tankless selection uses a different performance discussion, so a tank's first-hour figure is not a direct substitute for a tankless sizing assessment.
