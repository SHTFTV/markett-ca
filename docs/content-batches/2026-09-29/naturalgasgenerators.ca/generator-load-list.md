# Build an essential-load list before requesting a standby generator quote

Start a generator project with the things your household needs during an outage. A model number or a neighbour’s generator size is a poor substitute for your own priorities and an installer’s assessment.

## Separate essential loads from conveniences

Make three columns: essential, useful and can wait. Consider refrigeration, lighting, communications, heating controls, pumps and any other equipment your household relies on. This is a planning list, not a promise that a particular generator can power every item.

For each item, record its location and model, any readily accessible nameplate information, and whether it needs to operate at the same time as other loads. Do not open electrical equipment to find ratings. Photograph an accessible label only when it is safe to do so.

## Capture starting demand, not just normal operation

Motors can need more power when starting than while running. Generac’s sizing worksheet distinguishes running watts from additional starting watts and describes its worksheet as an approximation rather than a replacement for a detailed assessment. Give your inventory to the professional who will size the system; do not simply add advertised appliance ratings and order equipment. [1]

## Ask what “whole-home” means in this quote

Have the installer identify exactly which circuits are included, which loads may be managed, and what combinations can run together. Write down exceptions. Include planned equipment changes so the assessment is based on the home you expect to have, not only today’s appliance list.

## Include the gas side of the project

A natural-gas connection does not establish that the available supply can support a generator. Generac’s installation guidance identifies fuel supply and gas-meter sizing as part of the installation assessment. Ask the gas contractor to document the proposed fuel requirements and any utility coordination needed. [2]

## Keep a separate household backup plan

Canada’s emergency guidance recommends planning for electrically powered medical devices and consulting health professionals about medical needs during outages. A generator proposal should not replace that plan. Record alternate arrangements and emergency contacts independently of the equipment purchase. [3]

Bring your load list to the site visit and request a written summary of the proposed capacity, backed-up circuits, exclusions and next steps. That makes competing quotes easier to compare without guessing a kilowatt size.
