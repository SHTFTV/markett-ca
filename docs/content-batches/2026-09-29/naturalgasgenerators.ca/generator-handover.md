# What to ask for at your standby generator handover

An installed generator is only one part of outage readiness. Before the project closes, ask the installer to explain the completed system and leave an organized set of records. The goal is to know what it supports, how its condition is monitored and who to call when something needs attention.

## Get the system details in writing

Keep the generator and transfer-equipment model details, owner’s manuals, warranty information, contractor contacts and applicable permit or inspection records together. Ask for a list of supported circuits and any load-management restrictions. Keep a printed copy accessible if internet service fails.

## Have the installer demonstrate the agreed functions

Request a professional commissioning demonstration and a written record of its outcome. Ask what normal operation looks and sounds like, how an alert differs from a routine status message, and where the supported-load limits are recorded. Do not open a transfer switch or electrical panel yourself.

Canada’s outage guidance requires approved transfer equipment installed by a qualified electrician for connection to a home’s electrical system. A generator must never be plugged into a household wall outlet. [1]

## Turn maintenance instructions into a calendar

Use the exact model’s manual to establish service intervals and any requirements based on running hours. Generac notes that standby generators need periodic maintenance and directs owners to model-specific instructions. Ask who receives service reminders, who responds to faults and what happens if remote monitoring loses connectivity. [2]

Record completed service dates and observed alerts. An exercise cycle or an app showing “online” should not be treated as documentation that every backed-up circuit has passed an outage test.

## Walk through the outdoor location

Ask the installer to explain the equipment’s required clearances and how to preserve service access through local weather. Generac identifies site conditions and regional climate as installation considerations. Avoid adding screens, storage, landscaping or structures around the unit without checking the installation requirements. [3]

## Keep carbon-monoxide protection independent

Maintain working CO alarms according to their instructions. Health Canada warns that fuel-burning generators can expose people to carbon monoxide. Its portable-generator guidance must not be used as a substitute for the installation manual and local requirements for a permanently installed standby unit. [4]

Finish the handover with a short contact sheet: installer, service provider, utility and emergency numbers. Your household should know where it is before the next outage.
