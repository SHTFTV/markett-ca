# Before Replacing Your Heating System, Build a Whole-Home Upgrade Plan

Replacing a heating system is a good moment to look beyond the equipment itself. A cold room, uneven temperatures or rising energy use can have several contributing causes. Before requesting quotes, make a short record of what you want to improve so each professional is responding to the same problem.

## Describe the experience, not a diagnosis

Note which rooms feel uncomfortable, when the problem occurs, and whether it changes with weather or occupancy. Gather available energy bills and the model information for existing heating equipment. Record recent renovations and any changes you already plan, such as windows or insulation.

These observations are useful background, but they do not establish equipment sizing or prove a fault. Ask the contractor what further assessment is needed. Avoid selecting a replacement solely because its advertised capacity resembles the old unit.

## Consider an independent home energy evaluation

Natural Resources Canada describes an [EnerGuide home evaluation](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/product-energy-ratings/energuide/energuide-energy-efficiency-home-evaluations) as a process delivered through a service organization by a registered energy advisor. It can provide a structured view of home energy performance and improvement opportunities.

Ask the service organization what the evaluation includes, what information you should prepare, and what reports you will receive. The evaluation and the heating contractor’s installation assessment have different purposes; one does not replace the other. Do not assume that booking an evaluation establishes eligibility for any rebate or financing program.

## Coordinate improvements that affect one another

NRCan’s [guidance on renovating for energy efficiency](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/home-energy-efficiency/renovating-energy-efficiency) treats the home as an interacting system, including its envelope, ventilation, heating and cooling. That makes the order of your planned work a useful discussion with both the advisor and contractor.

Ask whether proposed building improvements should be considered before equipment is selected, how ventilation will be addressed, and which decisions require more information. Request a clear explanation of any assumptions in the heating proposal. A promised percentage saving without a stated baseline is difficult to evaluate.

## Compare proposals against your goals

Keep a small comparison sheet covering comfort objectives, proposed equipment, included work, maintenance expectations, warranties and unresolved questions. Ask for operating-cost assumptions rather than treating an equipment efficiency rating as your future bill. Weather, household use and local energy prices also matter.

If the current system has failed, explain that urgency immediately; the available sequence may be different from a planned replacement. This article is for organizing an upgrade discussion, not troubleshooting combustion equipment. Have qualified professionals assess equipment problems and any proposed gas or electrical work.
