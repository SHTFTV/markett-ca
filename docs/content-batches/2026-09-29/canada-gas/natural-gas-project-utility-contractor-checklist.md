# Planning a Natural Gas Project: Utility Work and Contractor Work Are Different

A new appliance can involve more than buying equipment and booking an installer. Your project may depend on work by a utility, work by a qualified contractor, and approvals administered locally. Sorting out those responsibilities early helps you ask better questions and avoid treating an equipment delivery date as an installation commitment.

## Start with one shared project brief

Write down the address, property type, existing appliances and proposed additions. Include appliance model information if it is available, your renovation schedule, and any future equipment you genuinely intend to add. Give the same brief to the contractor and the utility when they request it. Ask the contractor to confirm technical information rather than calculating pipe sizes or supply requirements yourself.

Keep decisions separate from preferences. “We would like a fireplace here” is a starting point for assessment, not confirmation that the location or existing supply is suitable. If your appliance choice changes, tell the people planning the installation before work proceeds.

## Clarify the boundary of each job

In FortisBC’s service area, its [new gas connection guidance](https://www.fortisbc.com/services/natural-gas-services/getting-natural-gas-its-easier-than-you-think) distinguishes the utility’s service line and meter installation from the licensed contractor’s work connecting the home and appliances. Other utilities may use different application steps. Use the provider responsible for your address, not a checklist copied from a different province.

Ask for written answers to three questions:

- What must the utility complete, and what information does it need from the contractor?
- What is included in the contractor’s scope, and which work belongs to another trade?
- Which permits or approvals apply, and who is responsible for each?

## Check capacity before scheduling additional appliances

An existing meter is not proof that every proposed appliance can be added without changes. FortisBC’s [service upgrade page](https://www.fortisbc.com/services/natural-gas-services/upgrade-your-natural-gas-service) explains the utility contact process when a gas fitter identifies the need for different metering or regulation. Let the qualified professional determine the technical requirement and coordinate it with the utility.

## Build a schedule around confirmed dependencies

Maintain a simple list of each outstanding decision, the person responsible and the next action. Ask which dates are estimates and which are confirmed. Before arranging finishing work around the installation, check whether permits, utility work and appliance availability are resolved.

This is a planning checklist, not gas-work instruction or a promise of service availability. Do not connect, modify or test gas piping yourself. Local requirements and an on-site professional assessment determine the actual scope.
